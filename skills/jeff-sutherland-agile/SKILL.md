# Jeff Sutherland Review (Agile & Scrum Delivery)

Plan or critique the way a goal is reached (a customer success plan, an implementation or onboarding plan, an internal project), using Scrum as lenses: deliver value in short, fixed cycles, review it with whoever the value is for, and adapt. The goal is to expose plans that are too big, too slow to show value, or never checked with the customer. Not to validate.

## Trigger

Run only when the user explicitly calls this skill or asks for a "Sutherland review", "Jeff Sutherland review", or "Agile review" by name. Do not apply it to ordinary requests, drafts or plans, even if it would seem useful. When it is not invoked, behave normally.

## Mode

- **Review**: the user brings an existing plan. Critique it (Steps 1 to 4).
- **Plan**: the user brings a goal with no plan yet. Help shape the first cycle: the cycle goal, the length, the few items that reach it, what done means, and when the review with the customer happens. Ask one question at a time, then write the cycle plan in the shape under "Plan output".
- Pick the mode from what the user brings. If it is unclear, ask which one they want.
- **No self-grading:** do not review a plan this skill built in the same conversation. Suggest a Sutherland review later or in a fresh chat, ideally after the first cycle has run.

## Step 1: Confirm what is under review

Identify the plan (or goal), what it serves, and who the value is for (customer, client sponsor, end users, internal team). If any is unclear, ask one question and wait.

## Step 2: Ground it in real context

Check whatever sources of truth the current project or workspace defines (its instructions, files, and any connected tools for tasks, meetings, support or CRM). Use what is there; do not assume any specific document or tool exists. Look for dates, owners, meeting history and what was actually delivered so far. If the facts needed are not available, say what is missing and ask. Label anything you cannot verify as an assumption.

## Step 3: Pick the lenses that fit

Choose one to three lenses that genuinely apply (in Plan mode, use them as a checklist). If none fits, say so plainly instead of forcing one.

**1. Cycle goal**
- Does each cycle have one clear goal the customer would recognize as progress?
- Is it tied to the customer's bigger goal, or is it just a list of activities?

**2. Value early, in small increments**
- What does the customer get at the end of the first cycle?
- Is the work cut into pieces that can be finished and shown, or is the value only at the end?

**3. One ordered backlog**
- Is there one list of what moves the customer toward the goal, ordered by value?
- Who decides the order on each side, and does the customer agree with it?

**4. Time box and cadence**
- Is there a fixed rhythm (for example two weeks) with dates the customer knows?
- Do cycles slip or stretch? What does that say about how the work is cut?

**5. Definition of done**
- What does done mean for this cycle, in terms the customer can check?
- Is it agreed with the person who will accept it?

**6. Review with the customer**
- Is the result shown to the customer at the end of each cycle, and their feedback used to set the next goal?
- Or is the customer involved only at kickoff and sign-off?

**7. Retrospective**
- After each cycle, what changed in how we work? If nothing ever changes, the retro is a ritual.

**8. Blockers and focus**
- Who removes blockers on each side, and how fast?
- Are too many goals open at once, so none finishes?

## Step 4: Write the critique

Use this shape, kept short:

**The blind spot:** the strongest objection, in one or two sentences.

**Lens:** which lens flags it and why it applies here.

**Evidence:** the facts from the sources behind the critique. Mark assumptions as assumptions.

**Cost of ignoring it:** what happens if nothing changes.

**Next cycle:** what the next cycle's goal, length and done should be, in two or three lines.

**Options:** two or three distinct ways to redirect, one line each, with the main trade-off of each. Do not pick for the user unless asked.

**Question back:** one question that pushes the user's thinking further.

If more than one blind spot is serious, lead with the most important and list the others in one line each at the end.

## Plan output

In Plan mode, end with the cycle plan in this shape:

**Customer goal:** the bigger goal this serves, in the customer's words where possible.

**Cycle 1 goal:** one sentence the customer would recognize as progress.

**Length and dates:** start, end, review date.

**Items:** the few pieces of work that reach the goal, ordered by value, each with an owner (our side or theirs).

**Done means:** how the customer can check the goal was reached.

**Review:** who attends, what is shown, what decision it feeds (the next cycle goal).

**Risks and blockers:** the main ones, with who removes each.

**Assumptions:** anything unverified the plan rests on.

## Rules

- No praise opener and no softening ("great plan, but..."). Be direct and respectful; critique the plan, not the person.
- Do not roleplay Sutherland or quote him. Use the framework, not the persona.
- Do not force Scrum vocabulary on the user or the customer; plain words work (cycle, review, list).
- Never invent dates, progress or customer feedback. If the evidence is thin, say so.
- If the goal itself is unclear or may be wrong, say so and, if a customer outcome or Jobs to Be Done review skill is available, point to it instead of repeating that lens here.
- Stay concise. A sharp critique beats a long one.
- Reply in the language the user writes in.
- The current project's or workspace's own rules (approval gates, confidentiality, tone) still apply.
